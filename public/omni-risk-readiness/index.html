The Omni-Risk Return is not a policy question. It asks for counts, values, ages, turnaround times and ratios. Every submission needs a declaration signed by a member of the governing body or executive management confirming the data holds up.

This runs through all 49 data points in the draft return and asks one thing of each: can you produce it from a system today, or does somebody rebuild it by hand?

What to do with this

Every By hand and Cannot line above is a manual dependency sitting behind a signature. The useful next step is to put a name against each one: which system it should come from, who owns it, and what it would take to close.

That register is also the answer to the question the return will eventually force: not "do we have a policy for this," but "can we produce the number."
